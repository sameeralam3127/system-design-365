# Content license

Two licenses apply to this repository, because it holds two different kinds of
work.

## The code — MIT

Everything that makes the site: `generator/`, `scripts/`, `assets/`,
`config/`, `templates/`, and the build workflow. See [LICENSE](LICENSE).

MIT is deliberate — the generator is meant to be reused. Copy it, fork it,
build your own site on it, ship it inside something commercial. No permission
needed, just keep the copyright notice.

## The writing — CC BY 4.0

Everything under `docs/`: the case studies, notes, trade-off write-ups, and
mock interview material, plus the diagrams in them.

Licensed under the
[Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

You are free to share and adapt this material, including commercially, as long
as you give appropriate credit. A link back to
<https://github.com/sameeralam3127/devops-case-studies> is enough.

### Why split them

Code and prose want different things. A permissive MIT license removes every
obstacle to reusing the generator, which is the point of publishing it. For the
writing, attribution is the one thing worth asking for — these are study notes
built up over time, and a link back is how other people find the rest of them.
